## Reflection

| Member | What have you learned? | How it changes the proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Dagohoy | I learned that even staff members tend to just bring it into their office and have printed paper posted up instead to announce lost and found items, and even then,  sometimes some are unable to do so. The staff even agree that a single system for a lost and found environment would be better. | It doesn’t really change the proposal much in terms of its features. If anything, it only strengthens the need and validates our current proposal seeing as how the staff describes the same features they hope a lost and found system would have. |
| Joash Casino | I learned that students mostly rely on retracing their steps and posting on the CATS group page when they lose something, and recovery usually takes at least a day since they have to wait for whoever picked it up. A recurring frustration is that found item posts often don't show who found the item, and finders can be unresponsive. Students are generally fine with item photos being public, but sensitive items like IDs should have the face and ID number covered. Above all, they would only switch to a new app if it is clearly faster and easier than the current method. | It mostly validates the proposal, but it highlights a few features to prioritize: each listing should clearly show when and where the item was found and who found it (or who is holding it), with a reliable way to contact them. The app should also include a search function by item name or description, and an option to blur or hide sensitive details like faces and ID numbers on posted photos. Ease of use and speed should be treated as core requirements, since that is what will convince students to use it over the CATS page. |
| Fontilla | I learned that the staff, a utility worker, picks up a found item and first asks people nearby whether they own or know the owner. If it’s the latter, she surrenders it to SID when she has free time. Currently, found items go to the SID right away. The staff member said IDs (important information) and the exact amount of money in a wallet should not be posted. In her experience, the exact amount was how staff verified a claimant. Her answer to the last question was indirect, but I take it to mean that what would make the staff use an app is protection and not just convenience. | For the claim approval, since SID already receives found items, we will assume that found items are turned into SID and that SID approves all claims. A status handover log with date, time, location found, who turned it in, and a condition note checked by SID at turn-in is a must for this project. This gives finders proof of when and in what state they surrendered the item.
| Gloria | I learned that, despite using only a pen-and-paper record system to track lost and found items, the SID still follows strict protocols when it comes to verifying ownership claims. It was also quite intriguing and surprising to know that former IT students conducted their OJT at the SID and even developed a system catered to the office. However, the major difference is that their system was exclusive only to the SID instead of available to all MSU-IIT constituents. Since the security personnel had already forgotten most of the features of the previous system, I was unable to note a few that my team and I could use as inspiration aside from being able to scan an item and its corresponding details immediately flashing on screen. They also emphasized the importance of the logbook as the key document keeping all lost and found records. Included in the information recorded are details such as what the item is, its observable characteristics like color and brand, where it was found, etc. These are the details that are then referenced to settle ownership claims. | Given that the SID already has a standard operating procedure when it comes to ownership verification, the system can use it as a benchmark for implementation in the digital landscape. This includes asking for the same details every time an item is posted, and then asking for information and even pictures to verify ownership. Also, given that the SID has already had prior experience with utilizing a student-made digital system for lost and found, it makes it highly likely for our system to actually be implemented and used by the stakeholders including the SID. We can even adopt the system wherein after 1 year of items being unclaimed, they are then sent to the WeCare office for use in outreach programs. |
